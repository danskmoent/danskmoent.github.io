Phase pre-I · 1618 → 1648 (Hede 25 series end) · Christian IV predecessor phase · court gift and mercenary pay
The Guldkrone tariff logic begins in parallel with the silver Christian IV Müntzformel via the åbne Brev af 12. Juli 1618[65]: Christian IV strikes 2-Guldkroner («dobbelt guldkrone») on the same 22-carat gold and the same tariff[61] as the later Frederik III form. Attested by the Esrum hoard (Ingvardson & Märcher 2010[66], peer-reviewed in Danefæ — Skatte fra den danske muld, Nationalmuseet, pp. 254–257): three double Guldkroner Hede 28, all dated 1628, found in the fields around Esrum Abbey and Møllegård (detector finds 2008 / 2011, follow-up excavation by Gilleleje Museum). Of the 12 known specimens of this type worldwide, the National Museum now holds 5 (≈ 25 % of the surviving corpus); Esrum delivers the first documented find spot for this type. Christian IV Guldkroner were not mass production for daily circulation but served as court gifts and mercenary pay.

Christian IV nomenclature: under Christian IV's own Pålydende convention the pieces carried nominally half the value of their later designation — the Hede 26 series (~2.998 g raw, .917) was struck as «1 Guldkrone», the Hede 25 series (~5.996 g raw, .917) as «2 Guldkrone», the Hede 28 series (~11.99 g raw, .917) as «dobbelt guldkrone». Only under Frederik III in 1655 is the same physical coin renamed: Hede 1957 records the shift verbatim in footnote (1) — «Betegnelsen guldkrone dækker under Frederik III den mønt, Christian IV kaldte 2 guldkrone»[62].

Phase I · 1655 → 1666 · Frederik III · Copenhagen 1655 + Glückstadt 1657–1660 + Copenhagen 1666
Frederik III main minting phase at the Guldkrone standard (.917, ≈ 5.996 g raw, tariff 3½ Speciedaler). Hede 1971 records two mint runs: Glückstadt 1657–1660 (f3h145; mintmaster Jacob Schwieger, canonical standard type) and Copenhagen 1666[63] (f3h44 for the 1 Guldkrone, f3h45 for the 2 Guldkrone at 11.181 g raw = 2 × 5.59 g). Tariff unchanged; the Copenhagen 1666 issue already shows a small rough-weight reduction (5.59 g vs 5.996 g) ahead of the formal tariff drop documented under phase B (1668).

Renaming of the unit designation in 1655: with the resumption of minting in Copenhagen Frederik III halves the nominal unit name — what under Christian IV had been called «2 Guldkrone» (Hede 25, ~5.996 g raw) is now «1 Guldkrone» (Hede 145B in Glückstadt, later f3h44 in Copenhagen). Physically nothing changes: rough weight and 22-carat fineness stay the same; only the nominal unit name is halved. Hede 1957 records the shift verbatim in footnote (1): «Betegnelsen guldkrone dækker under Frederik III den mønt, Christian IV kaldte 2 guldkrone»[62].

Phase II · 1668 → 1668 · Frederik III · Copenhagen · tariff 3 Speciedaler (tariff reduction)
A late Copenhagen follow-up to the Guldkrone series in 1668: tariff reduced from the Glückstadt phase 1657–1660 of 3½ to 3 Speciedaler = 18 Mark Dansk, rough weight lowered from 5.996 to 5.47 g. Fineness unchanged at .917 (22 carat). Reference type: KM# 303 / Friedberg# 92 / Numista N#308327; mintmaster Gotfred Krüger (Copenhagen 1664–1680), mintmark GK. Reverse legend «Dominus Providebit XVIII · M · 1668 · G · K ·».
Outside Holstein, included for completeness of the Frederik III tariff series: the coin was struck in Copenhagen, so it does not belong to the core scope of this register. It is listed here because it is the only other known piece of the Guldkrone tariff series and historically supersedes the tariff regime of the Glückstadt phase 1657–1660.

Standard relative to the Glückstadt form: at the same fineness .917 the rough weight is reduced by ≈ 8.8 % (5.47 g instead of 5.996 g) — fine-silver equivalent falls in step. From a Cologne mark of raw alloy one now strikes ≈ 233.856 ÷ 5.47 ≈ 42.75 pieces per mark, versus 39 at Glückstadt. Since the form does not yield a clean integer and the tariff also fails to match the 22-carat rough weight (3 Specie ≈ 18 Mark Dansk), the coin physically belongs to its own standard — the Δ column accordingly flags it as «outside tolerance» against the canonical Glückstadt form.

Phase III · 1701 → 1701 · Frederik IV · Copenhagen · striking for the West India Company
The last Danish Guldkrone: a 2 Guldkrone struck at Copenhagen in 1701 for the West India Company[67]. No specimen survives («kendes ikke mere»); the type is documented in literature only — Ramus p. 192, Wilcke III p. 20, NFM X p. 87[68] (Hede 30). Rough weight, fineness and tariff are not given in the available sources.
