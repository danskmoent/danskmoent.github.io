Period 0 · 1350 → 1392 · town coinage of Flensborg and Ribe
From the 1340s Lübeck coin spreads in Denmark; in the end it gains the upper hand over the English and the French[20], and sums of money are usually stated in «lybske Mark» («Lübeck marks»)[21]. The crown’s own coin was debased and lost all significance; the crown stopped coining, last at Lund about 1377[22].

The first coins on the Lübeck pattern in the lands of the Danish crown are struck by the town of Flensborg in the Duchy of Schleswig, in the second half of the 14th century under the South Jutland dukes and the Holstein counts[23]; as the legends show, the town itself conducted the coinage[24]. According to Nöbbe, the town probably received the right of coinage from the Holstein counts, who held it in pledge and set up their own mints at Rendsburg, Kiel, Itzehoe, Oldesloe and Neustadt; when this happened is not recorded[25][26]. In the kingdom itself the first to strike is the town of Ribe, probably from the second half of the 1370s; until about 1403, when coining began at Næstved, this remains the only coinage in the kingdom[27].

According to Lindahl, the legend MONETA HOLSASCIE and its variants on the Flensborg coins denote striking on the Holstein standard, whose exact parameters are unknown; contemporary records attest it as slightly inferior to the Lübeck one[28]. Flensborg coin is entered in the account book of the Lübeck merchant Wittenborg under 1359; in 1360 Lübeck and Flensborg «denarii argentei» («silver pennies», i.e. Witten) were held to be of equal value[29]. In March 1369 the Hanseatic diet at Lübeck resolved that money from Kiel, Flensborg and Itzehoe should not be accepted in the towns[30]. In 1374, 26 marks «Holsten ghelt» («Holstein money») were equated with 22 marks of Hamburg pennies[31]; in 1380, twelve Flensborg solidi with ten of Lübeck[32].

Period I · 1403 → 1513 · Rigsrådet · Hans
In 1481–1483, before King Hans, the Rigsråd struck Hvide[51]. No Danish Møntordning of King Hans has been preserved[4]. In the first part of his reign Hans had large quantities of Hvide struck, besides Skillinger and Søslinger, and also began to strike at København and Aalborg[52]. Skilling and Søsling corresponded well in weight to the Lübeck pieces; the Hvid was much lighter than the Lübeck Witten and passed for only 3 Lübeck pennies[13]. Under the Haandfæstning of 1483 the Norwegian mints were to strike «jaffngod mynt wid Danske penninge» («coin equal in value to Danish pennies»)[2]; Wilcke reads this as «efter lybsk Møntfod» («on the Lübeck standard»)[3]. The Rhenish gulden was reckoned at 2 Mark dansk at the beginning of the reign and at 2½ Mark dansk around the turn of the century[5].
