Period I · 1644 → 1669 · 10½-Krone-Fuß · Hebræermønt and Frederik III Glückstadt series
After the 19-year hiatus of 1625–1643 (see Christian IV's kroner above), the Krone returns in 1644 under the 10½-Krone-Fuß («Coarse Krone»): 10½ Krone per raw mark at 10¾ Loth (.672 fineness, 14.964 g fine per coin, Hede / Wilcke II p. 203) — ~8 % less fine silver per coin than under Christian IV. The series opens with the Hebræermønt (KM# 32, KM# 137) and the regular Glückstadt 4-Marck-Danske series under Frederik III. The tariff remains 1⅓ Speciedaler.[264]

The Hebræermønt is struck in parallel at two mints — Copenhagen (KM# 137, 1644–1646) and Glückstadt (KM# 32, 1645) — both bearing the Tetragrammaton יהוה on the reverse and both at .593 fineness (reduced from the Kronemønt standard of .671). At an unchanged tariff of ⅓ Speciedaler, this second debasement raises the cumulative seigniorage premium to about +27.6 % (see separate sub-table).

Alongside, the Glückstadt 4-Marck-Danske series under Frederik III (KM# A43, B43) at standard fineness .671 — the normative continuation that the Christian V series later carries forward.
