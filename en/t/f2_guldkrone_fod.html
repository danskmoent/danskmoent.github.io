Phase I · 1563 → 1564 · Klipping coinage 1563-1564 · .934 / 3.341 g rough / 3.120 g fine · beginning of the Northern Seven Years' War
A Klipping emergency-coinage emission of the early phase of the Northern Seven Years' War[56]. 17 October 1563 coinage verbatim: «17. Oktbr., af 66 1/2 Marck Kroneguld 4,860 Krone Klippinge.» (Wilcke Rentmeister account records, Kap. 6)[57]. Reference specimens Hede f2h2 (1563, unique) + Hede f2h5 (1564), Copenhagen / Bremerholm royal mint[58]. Reverse inscription «I GOLT KRONE». Coinage ends in 1564 (presumably due to limited Kroneguld stocks); for the remainder of the war (1565-1570) Frederik II shifts to silver Klipping emergency coins and to the imperial 9-Thaler-Fuß from 1566.

Phase II · 1584 → 1584 · Frederik II 1584 (Frederiksborg) · brutto 3.38 g · fineness not transmitted · gold gift set
A single coinage at the Frederiksborg mint for Queen Sophie's gold gift set, twenty years after the Klipping coinage ended. The set repeats the triad of gold types of 1563-1564 — Ungersk Gylden (Dukatfod), Rhinsk Gylden (Guldgyldenfod) and Guldkrone (this standard) — now as a court gift. Brutto 3.38 g; no fineness is transmitted, so the assignment rests on the gross weight alone, which comes within 1.2 % of the 1563-1564 target of 3.341 g (as computed from 3.38 / 3.341)[55]. The Guldkrone line then breaks off and is resumed only in 1618 under Christian IV with a different metric and its own tariff logic (Guldkrone-Fuß).
