Period I · 1618 → 1624 · Christian IV · Corona Danica · tariff 1½ Speciedaler
First period of the Christian IV Müntzformel: introduced by the åbne Brev af 12. juli 1618, «hvorved Kronemønten førtes frem for Offentligheden»[253]. § 1 provides «samme danske kroner geilde … saa høigt … som halfanden rigsdaller in specie … som nu belöber hundrede sex och tiufve skilling danske, en half krone for trei rigsort in specie»[240] — the krone at 1½, the half krone at ¾ rigsdaler in specie; the patent speaks of «danske kroner»; the name «Corona Danica» does not occur in it. All 1618–1624 issues run under the original 1½ Speciedaler tariff — a parity that in folk parlance prompted the satirical reread of the royal motto RFP (regna firmat pietas) as R(iget) F(attes) P(enge).[254] The Møntordning af 14. februar 1624 sets the krone at 144 skilling against a daler of 100[242]; the cut to 1⅓ daler comes with the Forordning om Mønten af 4. maj 1625, with which krone minting also ends in the summer of 1625[244]. Stylistic sub-groups: Corona Danica original strikes 1618–1622 (KM# 57–61, 58a, 59a) and «Dicke» / Open-Crown variants 1624 (KM# 94–99) — all within the same tariff regime. Wikipedia DE «Corona Danica»: «Die ursprüngliche Krone und ihre Teilstücke wurden nur bis 1624, teilweise bis 1625 geprägt»[224].

Period II · 1651 → 1652 · Frederik III · Christian IV Müntzformel continuation · transition to 10½-Krone-Fuß
Second period of the Christian IV Müntzformel: Frederik III (1648–1670) resumes Krone production at Copenhagen in 1651 and at first follows the older formula with 16.25 g fine silver, before transitioning to the new 10½-Krone-Fuß (.672 / 14.964 g fine) in 1652. Wikipedia DE «Corona Danica»[224]: «Friedrich III. lässt ab 1651 neben anderen Münzen erneut Kronen prägen, zunächst mit einem Feingehalt von 16,25 g Silber, dann von 14,964 g». Hede 1957[252] classifies the full Müntzformel as 1618–1652; the finest typology of the 1651–1652 transition is in Aagaard 2004[255] «Frederik III Kronemønt København 1651–1670».

Period III · 1665 → 1679 · Special issues — Pumphosenkrone (1665) and 2-Krone (1675)
Hede 1957[252] records two later special issues that approach the Christian IV Müntzformel's fineness, but which Hede explicitly classifies as specialised Schaumünzen rather than formula continuations:
· 1665 «Pumphosenkrone» / «Pumpbucksen Thaler» — Frederik III's Krone and ½ Krone (Schou 39/40), at higher fineness for a more attractive strike;
· 1675 2-Krone — Christian V's double-Krone strike (Schou 11–15) under the rescript of 11 March 1675 regarding the mint gain.
Both pieces are handled by Hede as «specielle udgaver med finere sølv for at få en smukkere mønt» — i.e. outside the regular 10½-Krone-Fuß (Grobe Krone).
